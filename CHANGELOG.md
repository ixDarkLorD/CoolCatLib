# Changelog
This file is for listing all the changes to this project

## v26.1.2-7 Release | Unreleased
### ✨ New Features
- Sound filters (`api.client.sound`): muffle and roughen the game's sounds, as heard through a wall or under a spell.
  - `SoundFilters.register(provider)` adds a `SoundFilterProvider`, asked for each sound with a place in the world as it starts and as it moves; music and the menus are never filtered.
  - A `SoundFilter` is how loud a sound stays, how much of its high end it keeps and how much of it goes through a distortion; filters combine with `then`.
  - `SoundFilters.refresh()` filters the sounds already playing again, when the providers' answers change.
  - Through OpenAL's EFX extension (a low-pass filter on each sound, and a distortion effect it is sent to); without it, sounds are left as they are.

## v26.1.2-6 Release | Oct 22, 2026
### ✨ New Features
- More common events (`api.event.v2.common`), fired the same on Fabric and NeoForge:
  - `InteractionEvents`: `USE_BLOCK`, `USE_ITEM` and `USE_ENTITY` (answer with an `InteractionResult`: `PASS` lets the game go on, anything else stops it there) and `ATTACK_BLOCK` and `ATTACK_ENTITY` (interrupt to stop them), on both sides, before the game acts.
  - `LivingEvents`: `DAMAGE` (before armor; interrupt and no damage is taken) and `DEATH` (interrupt to keep the entity alive), on the server.
  - `PlayerEvents.CLONE` (the new player object after death or the End, to carry data over), `RESPAWN` and `CHANGE_DIMENSION`; `EntityEvents.UNLOAD`.
- Custom boss bars (`api.bossbar`, `api.client.bossbar`): boss bars drawn a mod's own way, each in a style.
  - From the server, `CustomBossBars.style(bar, style)` gives any `ServerBossEvent` a style (and optionally the entity it belongs to); players seeing it are told, and those shown it later are told when they are. `EntityBossBar.of(entity).style(...).range(48).showWhen(...).show()` keeps a bar for any living entity by itself: its name, its health, the players near it, until the entity is gone.
  - On the client, `BossBarRenderers.register(style, renderer)` draws bars of a style, stacked with the game's own as the game stacks them. Renderers get a `BossBarView`: the progress eased smoothly, a trail of what was just lost (held, then draining), how fresh the last hit is, the linked entity while it is loaded, and the time. `TexturedBossBar` is a renderer made from one texture: a frame, a fill and a trail in its track, marks that light once passed (phases), a glow that pulses faster as the bar empties, a jolt and a flash on each hit.
  - A style with no renderer on a client, or a client without CoolCatLib, gets the game's own bar.
- Armatures (`api.client.armature`, `api.armature`): modelled, animated entities, block entities and items, made in Blockbench and played with layered animation.
  - Models: Blockbench projects (`.bbmodel`) are read as saved, with no export step: groups, cubes with per-face UVs, free-form meshes, locators and null objects, several textures and textures embedded in the file. Bedrock `.geo.json` with `.animation.json` work too, and an `.animation.json` alone is enough for clips meant for another model. Files go in `assets/<namespace>/armatures/`; an optional `<name>.armature.json` sets materials per joint (cutout, solid, translucent, emissive, glow), extra animation files, texture swaps, springs, head tracking, an idle clip, transitions, scale and `.obj` props. A texture named `<texture>_glow` is drawn on top at full brightness.
  - Animation: an `Animator` per entity, block entity or item stack, with `AnimLayer`s that each play one clip over the joints in their `JointMask`, overriding or adding to the layers below, with a weight that can fade. A layer can be driven by a function asked every frame (`layer.drive(ctx -> ctx.moving() ? WALK : IDLE)`), and `play` sets the driver aside until its motion ends. `Motion` says how a clip plays: loop, once, hold, ping-pong, a repeat count, speed (backwards too), a start time and what plays next.
  - Transitions: `Transition.crossfade`, `freeze` (the old pose holds still while the new one blends in) and `CUT`, each with a duration and easing. A layer has a default, rules for pairs of clips (`transition("run", "idle", ...)`), a `TransitionRule` function, and a switch to turn blending off; the animator has a default and a switch for every layer. A motion can carry its own.
  - Keyframes: linear, Catmull-Rom, Bézier and step interpolation; Molang in any value (`math.*`, `query.*`, `variable.*`, assignments, several statements), with the common queries built in and `ArmatureQueries.register` for a mod's own; `anim_time_update`, `blend_weight`, start and loop delays. Sound and particle keyframes play by themselves (a particle effect naming a world effect plays it), timeline keyframes run Molang, and `animator.onEvent` gets them all.
  - Procedural motion: `animator.spring(joint)` for tails, ears and capes that lag and swing (chains of them whip), kept out of the body by colliders, balls round the model's points (`spring.collider(point, radius)`, or `"colliders"` in the armature file), so a cape drapes over a back and the spikes on it; free parts that hang or float where the world puts them (a spring with no stiffness): `buoyancy` lifts one (a balloon on a string), `rope` lets its string go slack, `wind` drifts it in slow gusts and `twist` spins it on its string as it swings, all reacting to the clips that move where it hangs from (`"buoyancy"`, `"rope"`, `"wind"`, `"twist"` in the armature file's springs); and the world: a spring's weight kept out of the blocks round it and the entities near it (`collideBlocks`, `collideEntities`, a ball of `collisionRadius` pixels), bouncing off and dragging along what it touches (`bounce`, `friction`), so a tail rests on the ground and a balloon is pushed aside by a player walking into it (`"collide": true` or `{"blocks", "entities", "radius", "bounce", "friction"}`); a spring that `pulls` (`"pulls": true`) tugs the spring on its joint's parent along its length when stretched taut, by its share of their mass, so tension runs down a chain: a balloon draws its string of cord lengths taut toward it and the string curves as it is swung, instead of standing stiff with the balloon folded over its top; springs marked `bump` keep their weights apart and bounce off each other (`"bump": true`, a ball of `collisionRadius`, or `"radius"`): a bunch of balloons jostling; and `spacing` (`"spacing"`, pixels) keeps room between them, easing them apart before they touch, so a bunch of gently lifted balloons opens out into a bouquet; cloth: springs woven to their neighbours (`spring.link(joint...)`, `"links"`) keep the distance the animation puts between their weights, firmly when pulled apart and as softly as `linkStiffness` (`"link_stiffness"`, 0.6) when pushed together, so a cape of hanging chains side by side or a skirt of panels round a waist moves, ripples and folds as one sheet instead of parting into strips; and cloth sheets (`"cloth": [{"joints": [[...row...], ...], "wrap"}]` in the armature file): the faces of a sheet's joints are skinned, each corner weighed between its own joint, the one below and the one beside it, so a cape or a skirt bends smoothly between its springs and stays whole, with no seam opening or folding over, and their seams welded first: neighbours' corners that are each other's nearest and within a pixel are joined at their middle and weighed alike, so square-cut boxes that cannot quite meet (the panels of a skirt flaring to its hem) close into one smooth sheet that never parts; what a cloth is made of: `mass` (heavy cloth swings slowly and shrugs off the wind), `airDrag` (air resistance growing with speed: it streams out behind and settles), `flutter` (ripples the faster its wearer moves), `stretch` (gives and springs back), `bend` (holds its shape however heavy it is, critically damped: long curves instead of sagging into sharp folds or whipping), `follow` (its damping works on its speed relative to where the animation carries it: cloth keeps up with its wearer instead of lagging as if dragged; `"follow"`, 0 by default) and a steady `windDirection` on top of the drifting wind (`"mass"`, `"air_drag"`, `"flutter"`, `"stretch"`, `"bend"`, `"wind_direction"`), or all at once with a `Fabric` (`spring.fabric(Fabric.WOOL)`, `"fabric": "wool"`: silk, linen, cotton, wool, canvas, leather, rags, leaves, and cord for strings: a balloon's string as a chain of springs, skinned as a one-column sheet so it curves; keys after it change just what they name); springs are kept light: no allocation per frame, at most 8 sub-steps, a spring at rest with nothing moving it sleeps, block shapes are looked up a few times a second and entities every few frames, and past `simulationDistance` blocks from the camera (64; `"simulation_distance"`) a spring isn't worked out at all; `animator.lookAt(joint)` follows where a mob looks, or the nearest player for blocks and items; `animator.modify` changes the pose in code; joints can be hidden, tinted and lit up.
  - Effects: `.obj` props carried on joints and points (`ObjProp`), glowing trails behind a point (`animator.trail`), and effect particles pouring from one (`animator.emitter`).
  - Rendering: `ArmatureMobRenderer` (keeps vanilla's hurt flash, death topple, sleeping, invisibility, glowing outline, name tags, leashes and render layers; holds items at points with `holdItem`/`ItemSocket`, dims or darkens its glow textures with `glow`), `ArmatureEntityRenderer`, `ArmatureBlockRenderer` (turns to the block's `facing`, shows the breaking cracks) and the `coolcatlib:armature` item model type, with `ArmatureItems` for per-item setup. Anything can be drawn at a point with `attach`, and `ArmatureDraw` draws a posed armature in a mod's own renderer (`Style.glow` sets how bright its glow textures are).
  - Per-entity variants and see-through drawing in `ArmatureMobRenderer`: `armature(entity -> ...)` picks the armature each entity is drawn with (variants whose bodies differ, each entity's animator made for the one it uses), `tint((entity, partialTicks) -> argb)` tints it each frame, and `translucent(...)` draws it see-through so the tint's alpha shows (`ArmatureDraw.Style.translucent`; glowing parts unchanged).
  - From the server: `ArmatureControl.play`, `stop` and `setVariable` for an entity, a block entity or the item in an entity's hand, sent to the players who can see it. On the client, `Animators.of(...)` and `Animators.whenReady(...)`.
- Vanilla models (`api.client.armature.vanilla`): clips, changes and worn armatures for vanilla mobs and players, without replacing their models. `VanillaModels.player()` or `VanillaModels.of(type)`:
  - `animations(armature)` plays clips on the parts named like the joints (`head`, `body`, `right_arm`...; any spelling); a part a clip moves is taken over, the rest keep vanilla's animation, and armor and held items follow. A `root` joint moves the whole model (flips, sitting, lying down). Clips can be mirrored for left-handed entities (`mirrorLeftHanded`), and `torsoJointNames()` reads emote files whose `torso` is the body part and `body` the whole model.
  - Limbs bend: a `bend` channel (or `pose.bend`) curves an arm or leg smoothly at its middle, with its sleeve or pants layer, its armor and the item in its hand.
  - `pose(...)`, `hide`, `scale`, `rotate` and `translate` change parts every frame; `attach(part, armature)` wears an animated armature on a part; `when(...)` limits all of it to some entities.
  - `firstPersonArms(...)` shows your own arms in first person posed as the clips pose them, with what you hold, in place of vanilla's hands.
  - Other mods animating the same parts: Better Combat's attacks and weapon poses are recognized, and the clips leave those parts to them by default (`overlapRule(OverlapRule.YIELD / OVERRIDE / ADD)`). Other animation mods can be made known with `VanillaModels.registerOverlap`, and `VanillaModels.hasOverlap(entity)` tells drivers when one is animating.
- Scene viewports can lay text flat on their picture, in the texture's own pixels (`SceneViewport.text(line, x, y, colour, scale)`, `clearText()`): a picture of nothing but text is drawn once, so text can be shown as a texture and cut, stretched or bent with what it is on.
- Scene viewports can stand on a ground: `SceneViewport.ground(y, cells, floor, lines, shadows)` lays a floor with a grid of lines under the scene, turning with it, as large as what stands on it and a margin more, with a soft shadow under each entity.
- Event phases reach other mods' own listeners: `EventPhase.band()` sorts phases into `FIRST` (FIRST and earlier), `MIDDLE` and `LAST` (LAST and later), and events whose loader hook fires each band at its own place run `FIRST` listeners before other mods' NeoForge or Fabric listeners of the game event and `LAST` ones after them. `EventInvoker.invoker(Band)` and `hasListeners(Band)` let a hook fire one band; `invoker()` still fires them all, in order.
- `ItemTooltipEvents.MODIFY` does this on both loaders (NeoForge's highest, normal and lowest priorities; Fabric phases before, at and after the default one).
- `ItemTooltipEvents.APPEND`: lines added at the very end of an item's tooltip, after the loaders' tooltip events and every other mod's lines, with the player when there is one; not fired for tooltips an item hides.
- World effects can be drawn pixel for pixel: `VfxMaterial.withSmoothing(false)` samples the texture with nearest filtering instead of linear, so pixel art (block and item textures) stays crisp up close. Materials are smooth by default, as before.
- Config screens without Glazed Menu: `ConfigScreens` now opens CoolCatLib's own screen when Glazed Menu isn't installed, in the look of the screen effects screen (the config's categories on the left, its settings on the right, a search over all of them, light and dark mode), instead of returning null. Switches, sliders, dropdowns for enums, a color picker and text boxes for everything else; changes wait for Done, server configs are sent to the server when playing on one (or shown read only), and settings needing a restart are marked. Glazed Menu's screens are still used whenever it is installed; `ConfigScreens.isGlazedMenu()` tells which.
  - `ConfigScreens.categoryPopup(...)` opens a popup floating over the current screen, editing just that category's settings (with reset buttons and Save), instead of the full screen. A server config the player may not change is greyed out and marked read only.
- Quiet held-item changes (`HeldItemSwaps`, client): data a mod changes on a held item no longer makes it dip out of view and back as if swapped. `HeldItemSwaps.quiet(component...)` names components whose changes alone are quiet; `HeldItemSwaps.register((shown, next) -> ...)` decides for any change, from the stack shown and the one now held. Changes to attachments on an item are quiet by default; `Attachment.Builder.swapAnimation()` makes an attachment's changes play the swap as before.
- `GhostBlocks` (`api.client.vfx`): blocks drawn as ghosts with their real models (block entities too), one see-through picture so only their outside shows, tinted and faded by one colour; shown through walls, in the world as real blocks would be, or always; full bright or shaded.
- `HeldItemSwaps.quiet(Supplier)`: a component registered later (a `RegistryEntry`) can be named while a mod sets up; it is looked up once stacks are first compared. Calling `.get()` on it then fails on NeoForge, where components aren't registered yet.
### 🔧 Changes
- Block entity attachments kept in dropped items are written and read with plain injections instead of redirects, which the MixinExtras bundled with NeoForge could fail on at startup.
- NeoForge: payloads are registered as optional, CoolCatLib's own included, so a client with CoolCatLib (and client-only mods built on it) can join a server without it and the other way round, as on Fabric (NeoForge used to disconnect whichever side lacked a channel). Configuration payloads are only sent to clients that have their channel. `Network.sendToPlayer` skips a player whose client lacks the payload's channel instead of throwing.
- Rifts (`WorldRifts`) can be held facing one way (`rift.facing(direction)`), as a portal set in a wall, instead of turning to the camera.
- Canvas's namespace is `coolcatlib` too: its assets (`coolcatlib:bloom`, `coolcatlib:tint`, `#moj_import <coolcatlib:vfx.glsl>`, `coolcatlib:sky.glsl`, `coolcatlib:screen_effect.glsl`, the GUI and VFX textures), text colours in JSON and commands (`"coolcatlib:rainbow"`, `"coolcatlib:gradient/..."`), saved cutscenes (`coolcatlib:saved/<name>`, from `config/coolcatlib/cutscenes/`), translation keys, key bindings and payload ids. Its development commands are under `/coolcatlib_client` (`effects`, `cutscene ...`), and the effects screen's choices are saved to `config/coolcatlib-screen-effects.json` (choices saved by Canvas are not carried over). Shaders, resource packs and text that named `coolcatcanvas:` need the new name.
- The packages are still `net.ixdarklord.coolcatcore` and `net.ixdarklord.coolcatcanvas`, so code that calls CoolCatLib needs no import changes.

## v26.1.2-5 Release | Oct 14, 2026
### 🔧 Changes
- CoolCatLib is one mod again: **CoolCatLib: Core** and **CoolCatLib: Canvas** are merged into **CoolCatLib**, one jar per loader. Screen effects, skyboxes, world effects, cutscenes, GUI widgets and the render utilities now ship with the events, networking, registries and configs, so there is one file to install and one version to depend on.
- The mod id is now `coolcatlib`. With it changed: the Fabric entrypoints (`coolcatlib:common`, `coolcatlib:client`, `coolcatlib:server`, `coolcatlib:datagen`), the commands (`/coolcatlib config ...`, `/coolcatlib_client config ...`), Core's translation keys (`config.coolcatlib.*`, `tooltip.coolcatlib.*`), its payload ids and `ConfigTheme.STARFALL` (`coolcatlib:starfall`). Players and servers need the same version, as with any update.
- Nothing moved in the code: the packages are still `net.ixdarklord.coolcatcore` and `net.ixdarklord.coolcatcanvas`, and Canvas's classes keep their asset namespace (`coolcatcanvas:bloom`, `coolcatcanvas:vfx.glsl`, ...), command (`/coolcatcanvas_client`) and config files.
- Mods built for CoolCatLib: Core or Canvas have to be updated: the `coolcatcore` and `coolcatcanvas` mod ids are gone, and constructors are only read from the `coolcatlib:*` Fabric entrypoints. Depend on `coolcatlib` alone.
- Remove the CoolCatLib: Core and Canvas jars when updating: their classes are in this one. The loaders refuse to start with them installed.
- Maven: the artifacts are now `net.ixdarklord.coolcatlib:coolcatlib-<loader>` (`coolcatlib-common`, `coolcatlib-fabric`, `coolcatlib-neoforge`) and hold everything. `coolcatlib-core-<loader>` and `coolcatlib-canvas-<loader>` stop at their last versions: replace both dependencies with the one.
- Canvas's releases up to 7 are listed in `CHANGELOG-canvas.md`.

## v26.1.2-2 Release | Oct 3, 2026
### ✨ New Features
- Configured (MrCrayfish) shows CoolCatLib: Core's configs: every mod with one gets Configured's screens in NeoForge's mod list and Mod Menu while Glazed Menu isn't installed (with Glazed Menu, its screens show them instead). Booleans, numbers, enums and text get Configured's own widgets; colors, ids, lists and other types are edited as text. Synced configs are edited on the server for players allowed to.
### 🔧 Changes
- The config screens moved out of Core into **Glazed Menu**, a standalone client-only mod (`glazedmenu`) that reads Core's configs when Core is installed. `ConfigEditors`, `EditSlot` and `ValueEditor` are now in `net.ixdarklord.glazedmenu.api.editor`. Core's `ConfigScreens` (`api.config.client`) stays as a bridge: with Glazed Menu installed its methods open Glazed Menu's screens, and without it they open nothing (they return null; `isAvailable()` tells which, and `tellUnavailable(config)` tells the player how to change the config). `/coolcatcore_client config open` is now `/glazedmenu [mod] [config/category]`. Core still loads, saves, syncs and hot reloads configs and keeps its other commands; without Glazed Menu (or Configured) there's just no in-game editor, and a startup mismatch on joining a server is the disconnect message alone.
- The styled widgets and theme effects moved to CoolCatLib: Canvas: `ConfigEffect` and `ConfigEffects` are in `net.ixdarklord.coolcatcanvas.api.client.gui.theme`. The player's screen settings (dark/light mode, opacities, effects, transitions) are Glazed Menu's now, so Core has no client config of its own anymore.
- Core no longer registers config buttons in NeoForge's mod list or Mod Menu; Glazed Menu or Configured does.

## v26.1.2-1 Release | Sep 30, 2026 (Minecraft 26.1.2 port)
### ✨ New Features
- Runs on Minecraft 26.1, 26.1.1 and 26.1.2 (NeoForge 26.1.0.19-beta or newer, Fabric API 0.145.1 or newer).
- CoolCatLib is split in two mods: **CoolCatLib: Core** (`coolcatcore`, package `net.ixdarklord.coolcatcore`), this one, and **CoolCatLib: Canvas** (`coolcatcanvas`), which now holds screen effects, skyboxes, GUI widgets, `RenderUtils` and `Easing`. The mod id, package, asset namespace, Fabric entrypoints (`coolcatcore:common`, ...) and commands (`/coolcatcore`, `/coolcatcore_client`) all changed from `coolcatlib`.
- A cross-loader platform layer, so mods built on CoolCatLib: Core no longer need Architectury API:
  - Events (`api.event.v2`): `EventInvoker` keeps listeners in common code, ordered by `EventPhase`, and is found by its listener type (`EventInvoker.lookup(ServerTickEvents.End.class)`) or its constant (`ServerTickEvents.END`). `EventInvoker.create(Type.class)` makes a mod's own event, combining listeners by return type (`void`, `EventResult`, `EventResultHolder`, `boolean`).
  - Built-in events: server lifecycle and ticks, datapack contents sync (`ServerLifecycleEvents.SYNC_DATA_PACK_CONTENTS`: per player on login and after `/reload`), player join/leave/tick, entity load, block break/place, commands; client ticks, player join/leave, HUD rendering, item tooltips, client commands.
  - `api.registry`: `DeferredRegister` / `RegistryEntry`, `CreativeTabs`, `ReloadListeners`.
  - `api.menu.ExtendedMenus`: menu types that open with extra data.
  - `api.network.Network`: serverbound/clientbound payloads and sending. `Network.canPlayerReceive(player, type)` tells whether a player's connection accepts a payload (false for clients without the mod and for GameTest mock players; NeoForge throws when sending to them).
    - Record payloads without a `StreamCodec`: `Network.registerServerbound(MyPayload.class, receiver)` (or with an explicit `Type`) derives the codec from the record's components through `PayloadCodecs`, which knows primitives, strings, common Minecraft types, enums, nested records, arrays, `Optional`, `List`, `Set`, `Map`, `Either` and `ResourceKey`. Other types are added with `PayloadCodecs.register` / `registerFactory`; registering a payload with a component type that has no codec throws right away, naming the component.
  - `api.client.registry`: key mappings, menu screens, tooltip components.
  - `api.platform.Platform`: loader, environment, mod checks, folders, the running server, fake players.
  - Mod constructors (`api.core`): `ModConstructor` (both sides), `ClientModConstructor`, `ServerModConstructor` (dedicated server) and `DataGenerationConstructor` are written once in common code, each with a construct stage and a setup stage (`DataGenerationContext.addProvider` for data providers).
    - Fabric needs no Java entry class: list them under the `coolcatcore:common`, `coolcatcore:client`, `coolcatcore:server` and `coolcatcore:datagen` entrypoints, plus `api.core.fabric.FabricDataGenerationEntrypoint` under `fabric-datagen`.
    - NeoForge: extend `api.core.neoforge.NeoForgeModEntrypoint` in the `@Mod` class and call `common(...)`, `client(() -> ...)`, `server(() -> ...)`, `dataGeneration(...)`; the mod id comes from the container.
- `EventResult.pass()`/`interrupt()`/`allow()`/`deny()`, and `EventResultHolder.result()`/`getValue()`.
- A config system (`api.config`):
  - Declare configs with `Config.builder(modId, scope)` (typed values, nested groups, presets) or from an annotated class with `ConfigObject.register`.
  - Scopes: `CLIENT`, `COMMON`, `SERVER` (synced to players) and `STARTUP` (read before content is registered).
  - Types: booleans, ranged numbers (optionally sliders), strings (length/pattern), enums, colors, ids, lists, and any `Codec`; custom `ConfigType`s with their own editors.
  - TOML (default) and JSON5 files with generated comments; a config whose format changes converts its old file once (kept as `.bak`); broken files are backed up; `version`/`migration` and `aliases` handle renamed or moved settings.
  - Hot reloading of edited files, change listeners and `ConfigEvents`: `LOADED` (first read), `RELOADED` (hot reload, the reload command or `Config.reload()`), `VALUE_CHANGED` (each value, with its old and new value), `CHANGED` (once per batch), `SAVED` and `SYNCED`.
  - `serverOnly()` values that never leave the server; operators edit synced configs in-game with permission checks.
  - Generated config screen: search, undo/redo, reset, presets, validation, dependencies (`enabledWhen`), restart notices and list editing. Linked from the NeoForge mod list and Mod Menu.
  - `/coolcatcore config` (server) and `/coolcatcore_client config` (client) commands: list, get, set, reset, reload, preset, open.
  - `STARTUP` configs, read before content is registered (for item properties, which items exist...). Their values stay fixed until a restart; edits are saved for the next start. Joining a server compares them before entering the world: values must match by default (the client is offered to adopt the server's), `useServerValue()` values are taken from the server while connected, and `localOnly()` values stay per side.
  - Category popups: one category of a config in a small window with Save and Cancel, floating over the current screen or the game, e.g. `ConfigScreens.openCategory("mymod", "client/rendering")`, `ConfigScreens.categoryPopup(parent, config, path)`, or `/coolcatcore_client config open <mod> <category>` (with path suggestions). A single setting's path shows just that setting.
  - A color picker for color values (a popup with saturation/brightness, hue and alpha, hex input and dye colors), also available as `ConfigScreens.colorPicker`.
  - The main config screen (shown for every mod, even one with a single config): a header with the mod's icon (a "?" when it has none; the list of every mod's configs has its own icon), and each config as a portrait navigation card with its own artwork (faint, brightening on hover; CoolCatLib: Core's per scope, or the mod's own at `assets/<modid>/textures/gui/config/cards/<config name>.png`), its scope icon and a short label like "Server" (`config.<modid>.<name>.label`). Cards are centered, grow when hovered, and show a tooltip with the config's scope, contents, file and access. Mod Menu's CoolCatLib: Core button opens CoolCatLib: Core's own settings.
  - Page transitions: a config page's panels glide a little way into place (up from below when opened, down from above when going back, a shorter rise when opened from elsewhere) while growing from slightly smaller; the background stays still.
  - Choice settings: the arrows step to the previous or next value, and clicking the middle of the box opens a dropdown of every value (arrow keys, Enter and the wheel work in it). Constants implementing `EnumType.Displayable` can override `description()` to explain what they do, shown beside the dropdown on the highlighted value.
  - Config screen effects: animations a theme names by id, drawn behind the panels, over the whole screen, and over each widget (buttons, toggles, sliders, text fields, cards, popup panels). Mods register their own on the client with `ConfigEffects.register(id, ConfigEffect)` and use them with `ConfigTheme.Builder.effects(...)`, `ConfigBuilder.effects(...)` for one config, or `"effects"` in `config_theme.json`; an unknown id is skipped and an effect that throws is switched off, each logged once. The built-in `ConfigTheme.STARFALL`, every theme's default, draws soft glows in the accent and small faint stars falling from the top, shifting with the mouse by depth (parallax). CoolCatLib: Core's client config turns effects and the page transitions on or off (Effects > Theme Effects / Page Transitions); both are always off on the Fast graphics preset.
  - Redesigned config screens: a top bar (title, scope badges, search), a category sidebar, settings with descriptions and nested sections, and a bottom bar with the unsaved-changes status and actions; toggle switches, sliders, selectors and flat buttons; presets, confirmations and the color picker as popups. Icons are 64x64 textures in `assets/coolcatcore/textures/gui/config/icons/` (smoothly filtered), replaceable by resource packs.
  - `ConfigTheme`: each mod's own look, set per mod (`ConfigTheme.setForMod`) or per config (`ConfigBuilder.theme` / `background`): a full `ConfigColorScheme` (presets `DARK`, `LIGHT`, and `tinted(accent)` for the dark scheme shaded toward the mod's color), a background texture by its path (cover, stretch or tile), the texture's opacity (`textureOpacity`, letting the panorama or world show through) and the backdrop color's opacity over the background (`backgroundOpacity`). Players can scale both for every mod in CoolCatLib: Core's client config (Background > Backdrop Opacity / Texture Opacity). By default the screens are see-through to the title panorama or the world. Resource packs can restyle a mod with `assets/<modid>/coolcatcore/config_theme.json`.
  - Popup panel sprites: `ConfigTheme.Builder.popupSprite` (or `popup_sprite` in `config_theme.json`) draws a nine-slice GUI sprite as the panel of popups (category popups, confirmations, the color picker) instead of the flat panel, and `ConfigScreens.categoryPopup(parent, config, path, theme)` opens a category popup with its own theme, e.g. to match the screen it's opened from.
    - Toggle switches have an outline on their track: the panel border while off, a darker shade of the accent as they turn on.
    - Popups keep their buttons in a footer strip along the bottom and their content above it, so a sprite's footer band sits behind the buttons only (the color picker's Cancel and Done moved there).
  - The config list shows each mod's icon (a theme can set another); config pages and their cards show an icon of their kind (client, common, server, startup).
  - `Platform.getModName`.
  - Dark and light mode: a sun/moon switch in every config screen's top bar, remembered in CoolCatLib: Core's own client config (`themeMode`). Each theme has a light scheme too (`ConfigTheme.Builder.lightColors`, by default derived from the mod's accent; `light_base`/`light_colors` in `config_theme.json`).
  - The main config screen's search bar searches every config it lists (names, keys, comments, group names, values), with results grouped by config; opening a result shows that config filtered to it. A config's own search covers only that config.
- Attachments, containers and handlers for items, blocks and entities:
  - `api.attachment`: typed data any entity (players included), block entity or item stack can carry. Each mod creates its own `AttachmentRegistry.create(modId)` (and calls `register()` while it initialises); its values are stored under its own id, in the `<modid>:attachments` item component and a `<modid>:attachments` tag in entity and block entity data. Declared once (`ATTACHMENTS.builder(name, codec, default)`), then used as `MADNESS.get(player)`, `set`, `update`, `modify`, `reset`, or through `AttachmentHolder.of(...)`.
    - Persistent attachments save with their holder; `transientBuilder` ones never do. `copyOnDeath()` keeps a player's value through death (always kept through dimension changes and the End exit); `keepOnDrop()` carries a block entity's value into its dropped item and back when placed.
    - `sync(SyncPolicy)`: `SELF` (the player it belongs to), `TRACKING`, `ALL`, or any predicate. Only changed values are sent, at most once per tick; everything when a player starts seeing the holder, receives its chunk, joins, respawns or changes dimension.
    - On item stacks, defaults aren't stored, so stacks still stack.
  - `AttachmentEvents`: `CHANGING` (cancel a change or replace the value), `ADDED`, `CHANGED` (in-place changes included), `REMOVED`, `START_TRACKING`/`STOP_TRACKING` (a player starts or stops receiving a holder's data) and `RECEIVED` (synced values arrived on the client), plus typed per-attachment helpers (`onChanged(MADNESS, ...)`).
  - `api.container`: `ContainerLayout` (slot filters, limits, roles `STORAGE`/`INPUT`/`OUTPUT`/`INTERNAL`, which slots each face reaches) makes `SlotContainer`s, vanilla `Container`s with insert/extract, listeners and layout-aware menu slots. `AttachmentRegistry.container` stores one on any entity or block entity (saved and synced with the rest); `ItemContainers` keeps one in a stack (`ContainerItem` for items that have one); `ItemTransfer` moves items between any containers through faces.
  - `api.block`: `ExtendedBlockEntity` (attachment shortcuts, handlers, `serverTick`/`clientTick` with `ExtendedBlockEntity.ticker`) and `ExtendedContainerBlockEntity` (an inventory hoppers and comparators use; contents spill or, with `keepContentsOnBreak`, stay in the item).
  - `api.handler`: `HandlerType` for anything holders hand out (inventories, energy...), from the holder itself (`HandlerProvider`, `HandlerMap`) or registered providers and fallbacks for blocks, block entities, entities and items. `HandlerTypes.CONTAINER` also finds vanilla containers (double chests as one). `BlockHandlerCache` keeps a lookup until the block changes, its block entity goes, or `HandlerType.invalidate` is called.
  - Other mods reach these containers through each loader's own system, following the layout's filters, roles and limits: NeoForge item handler capabilities (blocks, entities and `ContainerItem` stacks) and Fabric's Transfer API (`ItemStorage.SIDED` for blocks, `ItemStorage.ITEM` for `ContainerItem` stacks).
  - `api.registry.BlockEntityTypes.create`: block entity types from common code.
  - Storage menus (`api.menu.StorageMenuDefinition`): a menu for a block's or an item's inventory, declared once (`StorageMenuDefinition.grid(columns, rows)` or placed slot by slot), registered with `createMenuType` and opened with `open(player, blockEntity, title)` or `open(player, hand, title)`. Slots follow the container's layout and shift-click; the menu closes when the block is removed, the player walks away, or the item leaves its slot (which stays locked meanwhile). `sync(attachment)` and `syncValue(...)` send extra values to the screen while it's open, and `button(id, action)` runs screen buttons on the server. `api.client.gui.screens.StorageScreen` draws any of these menus with no texture, and can be extended.
